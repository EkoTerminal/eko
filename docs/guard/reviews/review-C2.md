# C2 review: engineering feasibility, data and cost

Reviewed `brief.md`, `inputs/facts-and-requirements.md`, BACKEND §§3–7, 21, 23, every supplied code file, and both drafts. This is a static design/code review. Cost calculations below were independently recomputed with Python; no new RPC capability or engine throughput was measured. References to A/B mean the corresponding draft and section/task.

## Decision

Use B's data scope, optional history, stricter separation of control from asset provenance, fixed-exit calibration benchmark, and compatible wire levels. Use A's separate evidence/storage packet and finer separation of ledger, execution, depth, outcomes, scoring and history tasks. Neither build plan is ready to execute unchanged.

The principal blockers are funding discovery without a verified address index; historical and intra-block state reconstruction; calibration-window completeness; and expensive measurements being insufficiently separated from the cached evaluator. The request-price arithmetic is mostly correct. Small marginal RPC prices do not establish that the required history is discoverable, complete, or fast enough.

## 2. Recomputed cost, scale and funding feasibility

The unit price is `$0.000006/request`. At exactly 10 blocks/s: 864,000 blocks/day, 6,048,000/week, 25,920,000/30 days. These are planning numbers; actual ranges come from timestamped boundaries.

| Work | Nominal calls | Paid marginal cost | At 5 starts/s, ignoring latency/throttling |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| One-day full-block scan OR block trace | 864,000 | $5.184 | 48 hours |
| One-day full blocks AND traces | 1,728,000 | $10.368 | 96 hours |
| Seven-day scan, one method/block | 6,048,000 | $36.288 | 14 days |
| 30-day scan, one method/block | 25,920,000 | $155.52 | 60 days |
| 30-day blocks plus traces | 51,840,000 | $311.04 | 120 days |
| 82-million-block scan | 82,000,000 | $492 | 189.815 days |
| **Six-hour native discovery interval**, one trace/block | **216,000** | **$1.296** | **12 hours** |
| 100,000 selected block traces | 100,000 | $0.60 | 5h 33m 20s |
| 250,000 nominal enrichment units | 250,000 | $1.50 | 13h 53m 20s |
| 30-day logs, one group, 100k-block spans | 260 | $0.00156 | 52s theoretical |
| 30-day logs, one group, 2k-block spans | 12,960 | $0.07776 | 43m 12s theoretical |

A and B's quoted scan arithmetic is correct, including A's 2,080 eight-group lower bound of $0.01248. B's 190-day genesis value is correctly rounded. These are not total data-work prices. Trace availability on the public endpoint is not established; its 5/s time is a comparison, not a working public tracing plan. The spec says public state is short-lived and debug is unavailable; historical simulation/tracing needs the verified paid archive or a local node. Heavy-call wall time, payload size, retries, method billing and archive-fork lazy reads remain unmeasured.

At the observed 5.6-hour launch density, extrapolation gives 10,692 launches/day. Four early checkpoints × two reference sizes × EOA/contract classes = **171,072 probe jobs/day**, or **$1.026432/day even if each job costs only one remote call**. Actual multi-transaction EOA forks need additional archive reads. Including $10k at every checkpoint raises this floor to 256,608/$1.539648. The proposed $1/day incremental budget is 166,666 nominal units (about 1.929/s), so comprehensive remote launch probing alone exceeds it before funding/control/depth work. Use local verified math for ordinary Pons measurements; do template and sampled fork verification plus anomaly/on-demand jobs. Meter all upstream requests made by Anvil, not just the single local RPC that starts a fork.

The observed swap density extrapolates to 1,749,600 swaps/day or 52,488,000/30 days, and 320,760 launches/30 days. Treat these as stress projections, not forecasts. Fetching six profile fields per launch separately would be 1,924,560 calls/$11.54736 for 30 days. Static code reuse and batched launch config reads matter much more than saving a few log-range calls. Logs-first also needs token-address batches and dense-range subdivision; do not materialize 52 million swap objects in one replay cache.

### Required funding-plan correction

Replace A §8.2 and B §8.2 fallback language with this operational contract:

> The first-release native-funding adapter requires a verified address-indexed source for external and internal successful native transfers, with explicit interval coverage. Without it, a bounded shared block-trace scan is a diagnostic pilot only. It does not promise live candidate funding coverage. Scan each union of requested block intervals once, cache by block hash, and stop at the metered cap. Missing required recent coverage remains Incomplete/pending. First-ever funding and historical age are optional. No per-wallet full-interval scan and no swap-only inference of funding absence.

Why: all candidates on a dense launch day request overlapping six-hour intervals. The union rapidly becomes the whole day; three-hop expansion and proceeds collection worsen it. A $1/day cap funds less than two hours of chain-time block traces per day (166,666/10 = 16,666.6 seconds), even assuming no other incremental work. Trace-only can avoid a redundant full-block read when its output supplies successful top-level and internal value flows; capability-test that exact response. Top-level scans additionally need success status for included candidates. B Task 028's “otherwise implement bounded full-block discovery and supported block tracing” must not imply this completes the production fallback.

A's 10,000-wallet/50,000-call/$0.30 example is arithmetically correct but not a priced funding product. Two history pages may neither reach the interval start nor cover all internal calls; explorer requests do not inherit RPC pricing. Show explorer pages, RPC confirmations, trace units and provider charges separately. Quote budget caps and unknown subscription cost separately; neither draft can promise a complete funding graph at its proposed cap.

B's “significant current holder” has no numerical candidate cutoff. Adopt A's starting set: every observed buyer in the first 300 seconds, through the evaluation cursor, plus every current holder of at least 1% of S. Mark this CALIBRATE, retain excluded balance/recipient counts, and measure missed dispersed groups. Include candidate funder/collector degree-coverage jobs in the same budget. The 1% screen is a queue rule, not proof that smaller wallets are independent.

## 3. Cheapest sufficient calibration history

**Reject a 30-day whole-chain native scan and a full-engine replay over every historical block.** Keep B's complete launch enumeration plus selected-token reconstruction. A adds a useful explicit earlier history warm-up but its seven-day launch cohort cannot support its own 60/20/20 split with a seven-day purge at both boundaries. The purge removes the useful sample; A itself warns that the sample may be too short without resolving the selected dates.

Use this exact replacement schedule:

1. A code/coverage pilot uses the existing 200,000-block sample, plus boundary receipts/state fixtures. It validates arithmetic, acquisition cost and resume behavior; it provides **no** seven-day accuracy result.
2. Choose a predeclared **14-day launch cohort**, archive-backed if covered, otherwise prospective as B requires. Define development launches `[D,D+7d)` and locked test launches `[D+7d,D+14d)`. Continue observations through `D+21d`. Group repeated operators/components into test and remove their development samples, following B. Fit on development using purged internal validation. For one-hour primary labels, purge at least 3,900 seconds from development before the test boundary: max 300-second entry delay plus 3,600-second exit. Freeze before test labels are inspected. Seven-day label-based fitting needs a **separate** seven-day purge/expanded cohort; seven-day outcomes may initially be reported as held-out sensitivity only, not tuning inputs.
3. Enumerate factory launches over the cohort cheaply; broad launch history is metadata only. Reconstruct complete Transfer/curve/market/liquidity events for the 600 sampled tokens, their matched markets and incident controls, starting at each token/market creation, not at the backfill start if creation was older. Include the cohort's seven-day follow-up. Retrieve historical state at boundaries, then incrementally apply verified events. Keep sender enrichment on sampled/non-Pons paths only.
4. For current funding, fetch address-indexed intervals beginning one day before D, since A's chosen recent multihop rule needs 24-hour warm-up. B's seven-day recycling context, if kept, needs seven days of relevant candidate history rather than a one-day warm-up. Record the gap explicitly. First-ever pagination is optional and has its own budget. No address API → clustering/attribution stays unassessed; evaluate available execution/concentration factors in shadow.
5. Keep history booster disabled for the first calibration unless it has its own complete study. To evaluate a full trailing-30-day booster for the earliest launch D, enumerate prior launches back to **D−30d** and assess every eligible mature launch of selected operators through the feature cursor. For the 14-day cohort plus seven-day follow-up, this spans **51 calendar days** of launch/event availability, not merely “30 days backfilled.” No prior outcomes mature after the feature time may enter that feature. B's optional-history path is the cheapest sufficient initial release; validate the booster separately.

Lower-bound logs sizing for the 21-day cohort-plus-follow-up interval: 18,144,000 blocks = **182 requests/group at 100k spans**, or **9,072/group at 2k**. For an illustrative eight groups this is 1,456 calls/$0.008736/4m51s at 5/s, or 72,576/$0.435456/4h1m55s at 5/s. Real calls are `sum(ceil(range/span) × actual address/topic batches)` plus splits/retries, profile reads, receipts, sender resolution, funding and fork work. Sample-token discovery/filtering lowers payload and replay work; it does not justify quoting these lower bounds as total cost. An address-group count and measured density are required to make a final budget.

For 600 tokens × eight entry size/delay paths, entry plus one primary fixed exit is **9,600 quote valuations**. If each were one RPC, that floor is $0.0576/32 minutes at 5/s, before actual fork/state reads. A's 10-second one-hour monitoring ceiling is 1,732,800 valuations including entry, about $10.3968/96h16m at 5/s; B's 60-second secondary checks are 292,800/$1.7568/16h16m. Early exits may reduce A's count, but fixed 24-hour sensitivities add work. Keep B's fixed one-hour exit as the primary and run stop/take-profit checkpoints locally using reconstructed Pons math. Remote historical simulation only validates selected states or unsupported/anomalous cases. Declare paper quotes approximate; require matched fork accuracy before using them as ground truth.

Replay extrapolation from 17 minutes/200k blocks is **8.568 hours/week**, **36.72 hours/30 days**, before new metrics. It says nothing about native tracing, human review, seven-day maturity or per-state forks. The initial 600-token selective replay must publish its actual calls, memory, wall time and censoring before expanding. A 600-token double review is 1,200 human reviews plus adjudication; no engineer-session packet can “obtain” them by code alone. Keep B's explicit reviewer dependency.

Two benchmark definitions also need exact correction. B §9.1 makes a token hurt when half its eligible agents are hurt, while predictions occur at eight different entry snapshots; it never defines which prediction is paired with that aggregate label. This can hide a $1k-specific failure behind $100 paths. Keep B's fixed-exit method but use **A's primary 60-second entry, evaluated separately for $100 and $1k**, as two declared release targets; use the matching entry verdict for each. Cluster intervals by token/operator, never count the eight correlated agents as eight independent coins. Keep the half-agents token aggregate as a supplementary report only. B's 600-token strata overlap, as do A's; “deduplicate and refill” alone does not yield correct inclusion probabilities. Assign tokens to predeclared disjoint strata with a deterministic priority, save each stratum's population/sample sizes and probability, and sample by saved seed. Alternatively compute the actual union inclusion probability. Do not use a stratum's nominal n/N after silently deduplicating across strata.

## 4. Determinism, no lookahead and receipts

Both drafts demand pure rules and raw/versioned receipts, but the following exact implementation contracts are missing or inconsistent:

1. **Block-end versus event state.** An `eth_call`/trace at block B reads block-end state; it cannot supply state immediately before transaction 3 in B. Both drafts use event cursors for episodes and same-second launch entries without specifying this reconstruction. Add: “Production verdict snapshots are at completed block ends. Intra-block features/episode probes use a parent-block checkpoint and ordered transaction/event reconstruction through the exact cursor. Never combine an event prefix with block-end state containing later transactions. Unsupported reconstruction returns unknown.” Include a fixture with launch, buy, dump and fee change in the same block. Sparse log timestamps remain sufficient for windows; retrieve selected boundary headers, not every empty block.
2. **Known-at must be a cursor.** Keep A's full chain cursor. B's `knownAtSec` alone cannot distinguish evidence discovered after the decision within the same second. Use `(block/hash, txIndex, log/trace order)` for effective and known-at cuts, plus a persisted acquisition sequence/time for external evidence. Freeze the data-availability manifest used by a replay. Distinguish hypothetical rebuilt-engine replay from historical production replay. Do not map today's incident report/service registry/profile onto earlier production features.
3. **Half-open windows.** Keep A's `[t0,t0+5s)` and `[t0,t0+W)` conventions. B's inclusive first-trade-plus-five-seconds includes six integer timestamp buckets. It also moves the launch window if the first purchase is delayed. Use verified market-open `tp` (Pons launch time when opening is atomic); store first trade separately. Specify `(T−W,T]` for trailing windows. Timer events use the captured chain clock, even if no trade occurs at a deadline.
4. **Wall clock and external calls.** A's §3.7 freshness wall clock is acceptable for the served UI, not as an implicit scoring dependency. Set `evaluationTimeSec=cursor.timestampSec`; derive tax decay, cooldown, lock expiry, episode closure and snapshot-relative check ages from it. Persist `recordedAt/servedAt` outside the deterministic decision hash. Live policy can reject aged quotes using an injected captured request clock, as `preflight.ts` already does. It must record that clock; it is not a Guard rule. Fetch APIs/metadata/vendor labels only in acquisition jobs; save their payload/version/known-at/status. No `Date.now()`, current gas price, live registry, unseeded randomness or mutable vendor query in `evaluateGuard`.
5. **Probe randomness and isolation.** The old spec uses `randomAddress()` and a shared Anvil reset. Pin the ordinary EOA/contract test identities or derive them deterministically from the source snapshot and probe-class version; explicitly ensure nonprivileged state. Independent wallet-selective checks get distinct deterministic identities. Give each fork run isolated state or a serialized reset lease; concurrent resets must not mix sizes/tokens. Seed random sampling/bootstrap and snapshot hashes. Retain the actual overrides and purchased-wallet storage needed for delayed sellability.
6. **Math.** Use bigint/rational comparisons and a pinned decimal scale/rounding algorithm. A's `2^(−age/halfLife)` and B's log/exp pressure formulas are not exact rational operations. Keep B's step booster; compute log-product pressure with a pinned fixed-point implementation and precision/threshold policy, or store its normalized precomputed value with method version. Do not use cross-runtime `Math.exp/Math.log` threshold decisions. Score rounding is display-only.
7. **Version/cache keys.** Keep A's explicit measurement/identity/outcome/service-registry hashes and B's mode/parameter/code versions. Cache by token/chain, block hash **and event cursor where used**, evidence-availability cut, route/size/account class, all semantic versions/parameters/registry hashes. B's block-only cache wording is insufficient for intra-block states; A's derived `(token,cursor,all versions)` omits differing known-at cuts unless explicit. A new registry/profile/quote source/pattern/calibration status that can change decisions must invalidate the key and be receipted. Receipt hashes must include `rulesVersion`; neither rounded `cardHash` nor legacy playbook availability proves completion.
8. **Mechanism allocation.** Keep B's suppression only with a deterministic verified mechanism key: e.g. `(implementation, capability, effective-config-event)` for a tax; `(episodeId, attributed action IDs)` for realized flow. An unexplained hook discrepancy cannot receive a causal mechanism ID by guess. A later identified sale must not retrospectively suppress the earlier live exit factor. Specify all eligible suppression pairs in config, test them, and retain both observations. Do not suppress unrelated thin depth just because a sale occurred.
9. **Retention and append-only revisions.** A's compute timestamp can differ in the receipt envelope, but the same captured inputs must yield the same deterministic snapshot/decision hash. B's “every recalculation appends” needs a separate observation/run record or revision ID: the spec's unique `(coin,block,rules_version)` rejects multiple evidence revisions. Add a source/availability/revision hash to derived uniqueness; duplicate identical runs are idempotent. Reorgs append supersession/orphan markers. Thirty-day trace expiry is permitted, but a digest does not permit replay: retain normalized replay inputs/checkpoints, code/config and calibration artifacts for reproducibility, distinguish proof verification from full trace regeneration after expiry.

Contract choice: keep B's `clear/monitor/danger/pending` wire union and new display names, with additive `guardV2`/status-aware metric records. A's canonical renamed levels otherwise spread a breaking change through policy, Radar/Pair/Feed/Alert/BagReport, bots, WS, receipts and shared schemas. B alone does not solve `CoinCard.identity.deployer` being mandatory or its required numeric placeholders. Add a nullable principal/deployer representation in a versioned v2 card extension/endpoint; preserve old schema readers and never supply an invented address. Rename the new assessment check type to `GuardAssessmentCheck` to avoid colliding with BACKEND §23's existing trade `GuardCheck` (`pass|warn|refuse`). Add schema fixtures for unknown non-Pons identity and every shared consumer.

## 5. Engine throughput and what must be incremental

The supplied replay's approximately 170 evaluations/s is a **5.88 ms average evaluation budget**. It is not proof that either expanded engine maintains that rate. At one remote call per evaluation, public capacity would constrain it to about 5/s; even paid pricing at a sustained 170 calls/s is $88.128/day, before local fork latency. Keep all RPC, depth search, counterfactuals and review outside the evaluator.

Current `sources.ts` already uses cached Trade/Holder aggregates and lazily materializes `swaps/holdings` only for outcome/debug consumers. New code must not undo that. It still has work to remove: SQL per exempt wallet for old rug history; per-pool scanning of historical liquidity events; rebuilding cycling episodes in rolling actor arrays; legacy mutable `code_templates` writes from source loading. `outcomes.ts` scans token/horizon candidates and materializes full swaps; carry its replacements in a maturity queue, not on every evaluation.

Required state architecture:

- Per token: incremental exact supply/inventory state, balances with an ordered holder/group index, FIFO wallet lots, recipient-cohort totals, quote buckets, fee/config state, route/tick state. Update only touched wallets/pools and graph components; avoid reranking every holder or replaying all lots each verdict. Maintain reverse wallet→token dependencies for reclassification.
- Per wallet/group: running acquisition/sale/cash totals, open episode state, bounded cycling deques and expiry queues. Funding units are consumed once; new activity may invalidate materiality/expire links. Simple union-find cannot handle expired/rejected/reorged edges or splits; rebuild only affected bounded components from immutable edge snapshots. Do not delete historical membership evidence.
- Global: chain-time timer queue for probe checkpoints, tax/lock expiry, episode closure, one-hour/one-day/seven-day maturity and rolling-window removals. Timer handling must work across no-trade periods and checkpoint resume. Do not evaluate every coin at every one of 864,000 daily blocks.
- Execution: local pure curve quotes/inversion, persistent reconstructed state and cached immutable templates. Archive/fork workers publish versioned observations asynchronously; cache miss yields named missing checks. Actual order-size simulation is an isolated quote job; cached preflight stays in memory and revalidates bound state before calldata.
- Attribution: B's bounded episode mechanical replay is a queued worker. A's per-wallet pre/post/counterfactual cohort liquidation belongs in selected offline review, not every live verdict. Report queue age and leave attribution unknown while it runs. Recent outsider pressure can be measured without inventing operator responsibility.
- Persistence: bounded token/range shards, checkpoints and batched content-addressed evidence. Receipts hash references/normalized metric inputs rather than copying whole histories at every block. No all-30-day in-memory cache or replay expansion per evaluation.

Add a dedicated throughput packet. Gate on the same frozen 200k dataset and candidate revision: at least approximately 170/s for cached evaluation, zero RPC from evaluator, bounded memory on hot/large-holder tokens, deterministic checkpoint resume, measured aggregation/probe/attribution queue latency and cost separately. A regression must be reported or the live schedule narrowed; a pending result returned quickly is not evidence that full scans complete in five seconds. Keep <150ms cached preflight as a separate metric.
